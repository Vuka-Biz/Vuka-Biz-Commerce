How to spot fake food

Protect your township customers and safeguard your spaza operating licence.

calendar_month

1. Check expiry date

Look for rubbed-out, reprinted or smudged ink dates. Legitimate producers laser-etch or print uniform dot-matrix stamps that don't rub off with methylated spirits.

package_2

2. Damaged or swollen packaging

Never accept puffed tins, bloated dairy pouches or ripped seal liners. Swelling signals hazardous bacterial fermentation and seal breaches.

spellcheck

3. Spelling and logo errors

Inspect brand names, font mismatches, or fuzzy bootleg printing. Illicit copycats often replace single letters (e.g., altered iconic soft drink or biscuit brand marks).

science

4. Strange colour or smell

Reject chalky or greyish maize, discoloured cooking oil, damp flour lumps, or sharp chemical/industrial vinegar scents.

price_check

5. Price too good to be true

Unregistered unmarked bakkies/vans offering 50% below wholesale factory prices are almost always illicit, expired stock or counterfeit batches.
